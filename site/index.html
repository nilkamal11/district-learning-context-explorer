How to read the number

Zero is the national reference

On this SEDA scale, +1.00 means the district’s estimated average was about one student-level standard deviation above SEDA’s fixed national reference for that grade and subject. It is not the current year’s national district average, a percent, or a score out of 100.

What a change means

Different students each year

A change means the district-wide estimate was different from the chosen earlier year. It is not growth for the same students, a school rating, or evidence that one program caused the change.

The question behind the chart

Did districts that increased instructional spending per student also improve faster in fourth-grade math?

SEDA alone cannot answer that. Achievement, spending, enrollment, staffing, inflation, and district identity live in separate files. Even a correct-looking join can silently duplicate a district, attach the wrong year, or compare dollars that mean different things.

The analytical work is to make those decisions visible before a model is run.

The joined evidence

Five steps to one research-ready row

Each source answers a different part of the question. The crosswalk is the lookup table that keeps district identities aligned as identifiers change.

Concrete join issue 174 crosswalk records need an ID change

The released SEDA crosswalk contains 174 administrative-district records where the source district ID differs from the stable SEDA ID. That is why the join must use the year-aware bridge and produce an unmatched and duplicate-key report.

The analyst’s notebook

What gets decided before anyone interprets a result

Specification first
  1. 01

    Fix the comparison

    Keep grade 4 and math constant. Define the recent shared 2022–2024 window, the district population, and the minimum evidence needed for inclusion.

    Prevents unlike outcomes from being pooled
  2. 02

    Make district identity year-aware

    Join through the released annual crosswalk, then stop the build when a district-year key is unexpectedly duplicated or cannot be resolved.

    Prevents mergers and ID changes from looking like learning changes
  3. 03

    Put resources before outcomes

    Agree on the school-year and fiscal-year relationship before looking at results. Test a pre-specified one-year lag rather than choosing the most favorable timing afterward.

    Protects the time order of the question
  4. 04

    Normalize the measures

    Convert dollars to a common year, calculate spending per student, keep staffing definitions stable, and preserve missing or suppressed values as missing.

    Makes comparisons interpretable
  5. 05

    Stress-test the answer

    Carry SEDA uncertainty into change estimates, inspect pre-existing trends, compare alternate lag choices, and show how exclusions affect coverage.

    Separates a durable pattern from a fragile one

    Analytical specification

    Similarity first; outcomes later

    An explicit feature allowlist makes circular matching mechanically harder: no achievement estimate can enter peer selection.

    Outcome fields used: []
    District scale25%

    Log enrollment in grades 3–8

    Student composition25%

    Six race and ethnicity shares

    Place25%

    City, suburb, town, and rural shares

    1. Freeze context at 2024 and require the selected grade in the district span.
    2. Begin with the same grade-span bucket and dominant locale, enrollment within 4×, and poverty within 15 percentage points.
    3. Scale numeric differences on national 5th-to-95th percentile ranges; compare composition vectors with Hellinger distance.
    4. Select up to 15 same-state peers. Require at least 10 before that pool becomes the primary benchmark.
    5. Select 20 national analogs outside the target state, with no more than three from any state.
    6. If a stage must relax, expose the stage in the dashboard rather than hiding it.

    Uncertainty and interpretation

    The display can refuse to overstate a result

    Same-state peers

    Use within-state standard errors. “Higher” or “lower” language requires 10 reporting peers, at least 70% peer coverage, and a target estimate that is not low precision.

    National analogs

    Use NAEP-linking-adjusted standard errors and remain descriptive. Shared state-level linking uncertainty makes a simple cross-state significance label too strong.
